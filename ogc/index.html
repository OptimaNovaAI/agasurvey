01 · The Light Check for Legal Work

Two Questions Before
Anything Goes In

Ask First
Is it public, or is it privileged?
Then Ask
Does it name a member, a matter, or a person?
Green · Go

Public Record and Your Own Plain Drafts

Material anyone could read, and writing that names no one.

  • FERC orders, filings, and daily issuances
  • Court opinions, statutes, published rules
  • Formatting, outlines, slide drafts
Yellow · Strip It

Internal Work With Names Attached

Remove the member, matter, and people. Draft. Put the specifics back yourself.

  • Internal memos and committee updates
  • Contract templates and policy drafts
  • Meeting prep that names member companies
Red · Stop

Privilege and Legal Judgment Stay Human

Counsel decides. Copilot never gets the call.

  • Privileged communications and legal advice
  • Litigation strategy and settlement positions
  • Anything under a legal hold
Tap a light to open it

Two habits for counsel: check every citation against its source, and write prompts as if someone may read them later, because prompts can be records. On AGA's Copilot account, prompts stay inside AGA's Microsoft environment and don't train the model. Where this card and AGA's AI policy differ, the policy governs, and OGC owns the privilege line.

02 · The Prompt Recipe

Six Ingredients
That Cut the Editing

01

Role

Who it writes as. "You're writing for AGA's Office of the General Counsel."

02

Audience

Who reads it: the General Counsel, a committee, a member company, outside counsel.

03

Source

The one document it pulls from. Say "use only this, and cite the page."

04

Format

The shape: five bullets, a one-page memo, a table with docket numbers.

05

Voice

The register: precise and plain, no speculation, formal for a board.

06

Ask Me First

"Ask me clarifying questions before you begin." Then save the prompt.

03 · Move One

The Morning
Docket Scan

The daily FERC issuances and the appellate court report land every morning. Copilot reads the whole list and flags what OGC should look at, in the time it takes to pour coffee.

Green · Public recordFrom your wish list
Use only today's FERC daily issuances email. Flag every item touching natural gas pipelines, LDC rates, safety, or storage. For each, give the docket number, one line on what it is, and why OGC might care. Then list everything else in a single line marked "not flagged."
Before you rely on it: open each flagged docket and confirm the number and the summary against the source.
03 · Move Two

Inbox Triage
in Thirty Seconds

Give Copilot a time span, a filter, and what to skip. It returns the short list that actually needs counsel, earliest deadline first.

Green · Your own inboxFrom your wish list
List email since Monday that asks me to act, approve, or review, or that carries a deadline. Earliest deadline first. Skip newsletters, anything I've already answered, and messages where I'm only on cc. One line each: sender, ask, due date.
Save it once, then run it every Monday morning.
03 · Move Three

Sixty Pages to
a Two-Minute Brief

Orders, rulemakings, and reports get read in full by Copilot, then distilled into the five points counsel needs, each one tied to a page so you can check it fast.

Green · Published materialYellow · Internal drafts
Use only the attached document. Give me the holding or main decision, every deadline with its date, who it affects among AGA's members, and three questions OGC should answer. Cite the page or paragraph for each point. If something is unclear in the text, say so plainly.
Every cited page gets a quick look before the brief goes anywhere.
03 · Move Four

From Memo to Slides,
With the Right File

Type a forward slash in Copilot to point at the exact file, email, or meeting you mean. Then open PowerPoint and ask Copilot to build the presentation from that file.

Yellow · Strip names firstFrom your wish list
Create a presentation from /[your memo]. Eight slides for the Legal Committee: the issue, what changed, what it means for members, our recommendation, next steps. One idea per slide, short headlines, speaker notes with the detail.
Copilot builds the first draft. Counsel owns the recommendation slide.
